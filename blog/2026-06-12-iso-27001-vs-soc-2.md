Organizations strengthening their information security often compare **ISO 27001** and **SOC 2**. Both help businesses protect sensitive data, demonstrate security maturity, and build customer trust, but they serve different purposes and follow different assessment approaches. Understanding the differences helps you choose the right compliance path for your business goals and customer requirements.

<!-- truncate -->

## What Is ISO 27001?

ISO 27001 is an international standard for **Information Security Management Systems (ISMS)**. It provides a structured framework for identifying, managing, and reducing information security risks. Organizations that meet its requirements can achieve ISO 27001 certification through an accredited certification body.

## What Is SOC 2?

SOC 2 is an auditing framework developed by the **American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA)**. It evaluates how organizations manage customer data against the Trust Services Criteria: security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy.

## Key Differences Between ISO 27001 and SOC 2

**Scope and Purpose:** ISO 27001 focuses on establishing and maintaining a comprehensive information security management system; SOC 2 evaluates the effectiveness of security controls over a specific period.

**Assessment Method:** ISO 27001 results in a *certification*, while SOC 2 results in an independent *audit report* prepared by a licensed CPA firm.

**Global Recognition:** ISO 27001 is recognized worldwide and is commonly required for international business relationships; SOC 2 is especially popular among technology and SaaS companies serving North American customers.

**Compliance Approach:** ISO 27001 emphasizes risk management and continuous improvement; SOC 2 focuses on validating the effectiveness of implemented security controls.

## Which Framework Should You Choose?

Organizations operating globally often benefit from ISO 27001 certification, while SaaS providers frequently pursue SOC 2 to meet customer security requirements. Many businesses implement **both** frameworks to strengthen security and gain a competitive advantage.

## Conclusion

ISO 27001 and SOC 2 both support strong cybersecurity and data-protection practices, but they differ in scope, methodology, and compliance objectives. Understanding these differences lets organizations make informed decisions and align security investments with business needs.
